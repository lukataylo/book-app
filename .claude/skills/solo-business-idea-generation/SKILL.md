# Solo Business Idea Generation Skill v2

A pattern-derived framework trained on successful solo-founder businesses and refined against a held-out evaluation set.

The goal is not to invent clever products.

The goal is to discover businesses that a single founder could realistically build to meaningful recurring revenue by exploiting structural changes, distribution asymmetries, or newly-possible AI capabilities.

---

## Core Philosophy

Most successful solo businesses are not inventions.

They are combinations of:

* an underserved niche
* existing behaviour
* new technology
* excellent distribution
* obvious pricing

Novelty matters far less than timing.

---

## Stage 1 — Find structural change

Never start with an idea.

Start with something that has recently become true.

Examples:

* Coding agents replace SaaS
* Vision models became production quality
* Voice models became human
* MCP enables agent-to-agent workflows
* Local models became practical
* Browser automation became reliable
* Government released datasets
* AI agents can now spend money
* AI can use software
* LLM context windows increased
* Computer use models

Question:

What became possible in the last 24 months that previously required employees?

If nothing changed…

Stop.

---

## Stage 2 — Look for expensive human loops

Search for work involving:

* spreadsheets
* screenshots
* copy/paste
* searching
* reading
* compliance
* reviewing
* comparing
* checking
* formatting
* reconciliation
* scheduling
* emailing

Especially if people say:

"It only takes 15 minutes."

Those are excellent businesses.

---

## Stage 3 — Ignore giant markets

Large TAMs create crowded products.

Instead optimise for:

Tiny market × extreme willingness to pay.

Good examples:

* wedding photographers
* planning consultants
* tree surveyors
* mortgage brokers
* Amazon sellers
* independent recruiters
* grant writers
* immigration advisors
* Shopify agencies
* local accountants

The niche should be:

Small enough that nobody else cared.

---

## Stage 4 — Replace production, not management

Bad ideas:

AI CRM

AI Project Management

AI Calendar

AI Email

These coordinate humans.

Good ideas replace production.

Instead ask:

What actual work creates the output?

Example:

Bad:

"AI lawyer"

Good:

AI extracts every planning condition from council PDFs.

---

## Stage 5 — Look for hidden APIs

Many businesses are secretly humans pretending to be software.

Examples:

* research
* compliance
* reports
* planning searches
* insurance summaries
* accessibility audits
* data cleaning
* bookkeeping categorisation
* vendor comparisons

Question:

If customers thought this was software already, can AI make it software?

---

## Stage 6 — Distribution first

Never generate an idea without knowing:

Where users already gather.

Possible answers:

Reddit

Discord

Slack

Facebook Groups

LinkedIn

GitHub

Product Hunt

Chrome Store

Shopify Marketplace

WordPress Plugins

VSCode Marketplace

Apple App Store

Google Workspace

Communities beat ads.

---

## Stage 7 — Price from value

Avoid:

£5/month

£10/month

Instead price against:

Hours saved

Revenue created

Risk reduced

Anxiety removed

Good pricing:

£79

£149

£299

£999/year

Enterprise when obvious.

---

## Stage 8 — Prefer pull over push

Best businesses solve problems users actively search for.

Signals:

"template"

"generator"

"calculator"

"checklist"

"audit"

"validator"

"converter"

"estimate"

"cost"

"policy"

"how do I"

SEO compounds.

---

## Stage 9 — Look for repeated moments

Businesses around recurring events outperform one-off utilities.

Examples:

Every invoice

Every planning application

Every new hire

Every procurement

Every tax return

Every property purchase

Every code review

Every software release

Recurring trigger > recurring subscription.

---

## Stage 10 — One obvious promise

Users should understand the product immediately.

Examples:

"Find duplicate clauses across 500 contracts."

"Generate planning objections in 60 seconds."

"Check every Shopify app for hidden trackers."

Avoid:

Platform

Workspace

Assistant

Copilot

Operating system

Those communicate nothing.

---

## Stage 11 — AI substitution test

Ask:

If Claude Code, GPT-6 or the next coding agent existed tomorrow…

Would people simply ask it?

If yes…

Reject.

Instead build where you own one or more of:

* proprietary workflow
* proprietary data
* community
* integrations
* continuous monitoring
* marketplace liquidity
* operational infrastructure
* network effects
* trust
* distribution

If the AI alone replaces the product…

You don't own a business.

---

## Stage 12 — Agent-first opportunities

Prefer businesses where:

The buyer becomes another AI.

Examples:

Agent purchases APIs.

Agent purchases reports.

Agent purchases datasets.

Agent purchases validation.

Agent purchases monitoring.

Machines buy instantly.

Humans introduce friction.

---

## Stage 13 — Evaluate

Every idea receives scores.

Pain (10)

How painful?

Timing (10)

Could this only exist recently?

Distribution (10)

Can users be reached cheaply?

Monetisation (10)

Clear willingness to pay?

Competition (10)

Can incumbents copy easily?

AI durability (10)

Does it survive frontier model improvements?

Founder leverage (10)

Can one founder operate it?

Reject everything below 50/70.

---

## Stage 14 — Pattern extraction

Before accepting an idea ask:

Which successful businesses does this resemble?

Find at least three analogues.

Not competitors.

Analogues.

If none exist:

Either you've invented something brilliant…

or something nobody wants.

Usually the latter.

---

## Stage 15 — Force multiplication

Now expand.

Generate:

* enterprise version
* consumer version
* API version
* marketplace version
* browser extension
* mobile version
* agent-to-agent version
* local-first version
* open-source version

One core insight should generate multiple businesses.

---

## Stage 16 — Final vetoes

Reject immediately if it is:

* another AI wrapper
* another CRM
* another meeting summariser
* another note app
* another generic workflow builder
* another chatbot
* another "chat with your…"
* another personal finance dashboard
* another productivity app
* another AI search engine
* another "second brain"

Unless there is a structural breakthrough that makes it fundamentally different.

---

## Stage 17 — The "2030 Test"

Imagine it is 2030.

Frontier AI is effectively free.

Coding agents are ubiquitous.

Voice, vision, reasoning, and browser use are commodities.

Ask:

What scarce asset remains?

The strongest businesses own one or more of:

* Exclusive data pipelines
* Distribution channels
* Trusted brand
* Regulatory position
* Community
* Network effects
* Operational execution
* Physical-world integration
* Proprietary feedback loops

The business should become more valuable as AI improves, not less.

---

## Final Prompt

For every opportunity, output:

1. One-sentence idea.
2. Structural change enabling it.
3. Customer.
4. Pain.
5. Existing workaround.
6. Why now.
7. Why AI alone won't replace it.
8. Distribution channel.
9. Pricing.
10. MVP in one week.
11. 12-month roadmap.
12. Defensibility.
13. Three successful analogues.
14. Biggest risk.
15. Overall score out of 70.
