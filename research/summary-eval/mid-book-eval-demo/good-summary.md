Crime and Punishment follows Raskolnikov, an impoverished former student in St. Petersburg, who
murders the pawnbroker Alyona Ivanovna with an axe — and, when she returns unannounced, her
half-sister Lizaveta too. The next morning a police summons for an unrelated debt nearly breaks
him before he even reaches the door. He meets Marmeladov, whose daughter Sonya has turned to
prostitution to feed the family; when Marmeladov is crushed by a carriage, Raskolnikov gives away
his last money at the funeral. A letter from his mother describes Dunya's engagement to the
lawyer Luzhin and her earlier, uglier entanglement with her former employer Svidrigailov.

The middle of the novel is carried by three threads running in parallel. Svidrigailov arrives in
Petersburg, tells Raskolnikov that his dead wife Marfa Petrovna's ghost has visited him three
times, and reveals — chillingly — that he has been renting the room next door to Sonya and
overheard Raskolnikov's confession to her through the wall. Separately, the examining magistrate
Porfiry Petrovich runs Raskolnikov through a slow, needling interrogation, never accusing him
outright but making clear he suspects everything. And Luzhin, humiliated and cut loose by Dunya,
takes his revenge by secretly planting a 100-rouble note in Sonya's pocket at the Marmeladov
memorial dinner and then publicly accusing her of theft — a scheme unraveled on the spot by a
witness who saw him do it. Svidrigailov later offers Dunya 10,000 roubles to break off her
engagement; she refuses in fury and orders him out.

Before his suicide, Svidrigailov gives Sonya 3,000 roubles, funding the journey she'll make to
Siberia once Raskolnikov, prompted at last by Sonya, confesses at the police station. He's
sentenced to eight years of penal servitude. In the epilogue's delirious dream of a plague of
self-righteous "trichinae," Dostoevsky stages the novel's final turn: Raskolnikov's slow
movement, through Sonya, toward the Gospels she has carried since Part I.
