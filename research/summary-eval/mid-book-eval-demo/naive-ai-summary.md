Crime and Punishment follows Raskolnikov, an impoverished former student in St. Petersburg,
who murders an old pawnbroker with an axe, believing himself exempt from ordinary morality.
He also kills her sister Lizaveta, who arrives unexpectedly. Wracked by guilt, he wanders the
city in a feverish daze. He meets Marmeladov, a broken alcoholic whose daughter Sonya has been
forced into prostitution, and later gives away his last money at the man's funeral after he's
run over in the street. A letter from his mother explains that his sister Dunya is engaged to
a calculating lawyer.

In the middle chapters, Raskolnikov becomes entangled in a series of increasingly tense
encounters as suspicion mounts around him and various characters pursue their own agendas. He
grows closer to Sonya even as his paranoia deepens, and the net of the investigation slowly
tightens. Old acquaintances resurface, family tensions escalate, and money and reputation
become sources of ongoing conflict, all while Raskolnikov's psychological unraveling continues
beneath the surface of these events.

Eventually, worn down by guilt and Sonya's quiet moral influence, Raskolnikov confesses to the
police and is sentenced to eight years of hard labor in Siberia, where Sonya follows him. In the
epilogue he has a strange fever dream about an ideological plague, and begins, at last, to move
toward spiritual redemption through his love for her.
